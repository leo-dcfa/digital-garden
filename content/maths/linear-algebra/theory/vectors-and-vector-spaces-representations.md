# Vectors and Vector Spaces

![](vectors-and-vector-spaces-representations.png)

## Algebraic Representations

e.g. $(1, 3)$, $\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)$, $(0, 0, 0)$

**Ordered n-tuple:** given a positive integer $n$, an ordered sequence of $n$ real (or complex) numbers is called an ordered n-tuple.

**Euclidean space:** the set of all n-tuples of real numbers is called n-space or Euclidean space, denoted $\mathbb{R}^n$.

- the set of all n-tuples of complex numbers is called complex Euclidean space, denoted $\mathbb{C}^n$.

A vector in $\mathbb{R}^n$ or $\mathbb{C}^n$ can be represented as an n-tuple

$$v = \langle v_1, v_2, v_3 \ldots v_n \rangle$$

where $v_i \in \mathbb{R}$ if $v \in \mathbb{R}^n$, or $v_i \in \mathbb{C}$ if $v \in \mathbb{C}^n$ for $i = 1, 2, \ldots, n$. This is known as the algebraic representation of a vector. Each number is called a component or coordinate.

Zero vector $\Rightarrow 0 = \langle 0, 0, 0, \ldots, 0 \rangle$

- A vector in $\mathbb{R}^n$ is called a real vector
- A vector in $\mathbb{C}^n$ is called a complex vector

## Geometric Representation

- Real vectors in $\mathbb{R}^2$ and $\mathbb{R}^3$ can be represented geometrically
- Commonly used in physics where quantities possess magnitude and direction

A vector $v$ is drawn as an arrow from an **initial point** to a **terminal point**.

**Collinear vectors** and **parallel vectors**.

A zero geometric vector has coincident initial and terminal points. It has a length of zero and no natural direction.
